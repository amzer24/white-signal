# WHITE SIGNAL: progression for an exploration-first game

Research note, 13 September 2026. Scope: progression architecture, rewards, fairness and persistence; no implementation approval or game edits. The user's explicit direction is **exploration first: lasting abilities, discoveries and interconnected places**. All proposals below are hypotheses serving that direction. The current five-shard drafts and any staged replacement-card or 8/12/16 pacing experiments are not recommendations approved by this research.

## Finding: choose the promise before tuning its economy

The supplied current-system inventory has ten glyphs, normal capacity three in the tutorial then four, and curse capacity two. Dash and jump each cost two normal slots; feather, swift, spring, magnet, aegis and stomp each cost one; heavy and glass curses cost one and two. Every five shards presents three random unowned glyphs. The first three playable relays contain 69 shards, enough for thirteen threshold crossings if collected. These are project-context facts supplied to this investigation, not independently audited source-code findings.

There are two different exhaustion problems. Ownership exhausts when the finite catalogue is collected. Configuration exhausts when the player has an attractive legal build and later offers cannot improve or meaningfully vary it. Thirteen nominal reward occasions against ten unique acquisitions already create pressure, but increasing prices addresses neither the meaning of a reward nor the value of a place. Some players also collect fewer than 69 shards. A single total therefore cannot establish experienced pacing.

Exploration-first supplies the missing priority: a reward should help the player understand, reach or transform somewhere. Cards can remain a presentation format, but random compulsory acquisition is no longer the organising principle. A glyph discovered at a recognisable ruin can be compatible with this world; an unrelated draft interrupting a difficult jump every five pickups may weaken the connection between discovery and reward. That latter judgment is a design hypothesis to test, not a proven universal response.

## What the primary evidence supports

**Hollow Knight distinguishes collection from equipment capacity.** Team Cherry's pre-release charm article describes charms found in treasure chambers, behind creatures and through characters; they are equipped at benches, have different notch costs, and notch capacity can expand. Its examples span collection convenience, navigation, damage and risk. This directly supports a spatially grounded collection with restricted simultaneous use. It does not establish that every movement ability should occupy a charm slot. The official game page separately promotes interconnected exploration and new abilities. Its exact permanent traversal/save rules are not documented by these pages, so this note does not claim a complete mechanical audit. For WHITE SIGNAL, the useful borrowing is ownership versus configuration, rather than reproducing notch counts. [Team Cherry, “Revealing the Power of the Charms”](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/revealing-the-power-of-the-charms); [official Hollow Knight page](https://www.hollowknight.com/).

**Ori offers a similar distinction in first-party language.** Xbox's March 2020 launch article describes spirit weapons and skills alongside a shard system, and explicitly describes swapping Spirit Shard loadouts. It also offers Spirit Trials and Spirit Shrines as different challenge/reward activities. That supports separating acquired capabilities, optional customisation and mastery rewards. It does not verify individual ability-tree ordering, precise slot counts, or all death/save behaviour. Treat the common claim that Ori proves a particular numerical shard design as stronger than this evidence permits. WHITE SIGNAL can borrow the separation without inheriting an entire combat economy. [Xbox Wire, “Ori and the Will of the Wisps Available Now,” 11 March 2020](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2020/03/11/ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-available-now/).

**Dead Cells explains why the reset contract matters.** Motion Twin's official description makes repeated attempts and permadeath central; its publisher-authored Steam description says some unlocked paths, levels, mutations, abilities and weapons carry progress into successive runs. This supports a two-timescale architecture: the present attempt and future possibilities. Historical official patch notes also show cells funding Forge drop-quality investment and fixing inaccessible locations, including a transition that trapped players without the spider rune. These are evidence of actual systems and corrections, not a statement of original intent. Random builds still require reachable routes. Copying the draft while omitting the repeated-run purpose would transfer the reward mechanism without its supporting contract. [Dead Cells official site](https://dead-cells.com/); [Motion Twin's Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/588650/Dead_Cells/); [official Update 12 notes](https://deadcells.com/patchnotes/12).

**Metroid ties improvement to new environmental relationships.** Nintendo's Dread report explains that Grapple Beam both moves objects and reaches previously inaccessible places, while missile tanks increase ammunition capacity. Its developers explicitly say that actions were designed to preserve speed and momentum. Functional tools and quantitative upgrades consequently need not perform the same job. WHITE SIGNAL can place an important traversal discovery where its new interaction is immediately legible, then use it to reconnect to a remembered place. This is a proposal derived from the documented mechanics, not a claim that Nintendo prescribes this exact reward sequence. [Nintendo, “Metroid Dread Report Vol. 5: The powers of Samus Aran,” 20 August 2021](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2021/August/Metroid-Dread-Report-Vol-5-The-powers-of-Samus-Aran-2026540.html).

**Zelda also supplies a useful counterexample to permanent tools.** In Nintendo's A Link Between Worlds interview, the developers explain rental items returning after game over, the tension that creates, and the importance of preserving motivation to retry through easy borrowing and warp points. This is explicit creator reasoning, but specific to that game. It demonstrates that tool loss cannot be evaluated separately from recovery friction. WHITE SIGNAL's lasting-discovery promise argues against routine traversal-tool loss; a temporary optional expedition could use it only with clearly stated scope and a fast recovery route. [Nintendo, “Iwata Asks: A Link Between Worlds — 7. A Challenge from the Developers”](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-A-Link-Between-Worlds/The-Legend-of-Zelda-A-Link-Between-Worlds/7-A-Challenge-from-the-Developers/7-A-Challenge-from-the-Developers-832213.html).

**Celeste demonstrates capability expressed through surroundings.** Noel Berry's published Player.cs contains refill, bounce and booster interactions: boost entry refills dash and stamina; bounce handling can refill them; normal grounded logic can restore dash subject to conditions. This is narrow but direct evidence that local encounters can renew and reshape movement without collecting another permanent inventory object. Maddy Thorson's own forgiveness article describes timing and positioning allowances and explicitly connects them with making difficulty feel kind. WHITE SIGNAL should therefore test environmental Signal interactions and preserve forgiving movement independently of reward inflation. The GDC 2017 design-talk page was read, but its video/transcript was not accessible through this research interface; no detailed talk claim rests on its abstract. [official Player.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs); [“Celeste & Forgiveness”](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html); [GDC session record](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024307/Level-Design-Workshop-Designing-Celeste).

## Three coherent architectures

### A. Lasting toolkit and transformed world — strongest baseline

Major abilities are earned at authored discoveries or demonstrated mastery, remain permanently available, and have no equipment-capacity cost. Relays restore persistent world functions: a passage opens, interference clears, or a route reconnects. Shards support optional purchases, restoration or information, rather than randomly deciding whether the player can continue.

Motivations are curiosity, remembered obstacles, competence and visible consequence. Rewards can alternate between verbs, shortcuts, lore with navigational relevance, optional challenge access, modest resource expansion and striking discoveries. Eight relays do not imply eight new buttons: one can teach, another deepen an interaction, another reconnect areas, and another combine familiar mechanics.

Capacity belongs only to expendable resources if needed; it does not restrict essential traversal. Save milestones, abilities, opened shortcuts and unique collection flags together. Death returns the player to an established checkpoint with discoveries intact; exact health and carried-resource penalties remain a test question. Route fairness is straightforward: every required obstacle is solvable with the guaranteed toolkit, with visible hints for deliberate future gates. The production cost is meaningful world reuse and readable interconnections. Simply making glyphs permanent while keeping a corridor of unrelated stages would not deliver the stated promise.

### B. Run builds with persistent unlocks — coherent, but wrong foundation here

A bounded expedition resets its equipped modifiers, while discoveries expand a persistent catalogue. Random rewards ask players to adapt; fixed capacity produces opportunity costs. Milestones are bosses, depth, successful extraction or completion of the run. The save system distinguishes suspending an attempt from ending it; death resets exactly the advertised attempt state while retaining specified meta-progress.

This structure makes repeated reward offers meaningful again, but demands attractive replay, sufficient build variety and clear reset stakes. Mandatory movement must remain available or generation must guarantee an alternate route. Repeating the same authored traversal to reroll a necessary ability is a fairness failure, not build depth. Its reward is improvisation under temporary conditions, whereas the user has chosen durable knowledge of a world. Retain it only as a possible optional postgame challenge, not as the main campaign's unresolved alternative.

### C. Lasting toolkit plus optional Signal attunements — promising conditional layer

Use architecture A for all required routes, then let discovered glyphs alter optional style. Attunements might change protection, pickup convenience or a specialised interaction. They are permanently owned and changed at relays. Their capacity constrains simultaneous advantages, while required traversal remains independent. Shards can restore a discovered attunement or fund an optional upgrade selected from known choices.

Motivation combines exploration with experimentation. Milestones can earn a new attunement or occasional capacity expansion, but a full loadout remains a valid state: later discoveries need not always increase power. Death preserves ownership and the equipped configuration; relay changes save normally. The main risk is that speed/jump modifiers silently become route requirements or clearly superior defaults. If most players never swap, remove the layer or make choices more situational; do not manufacture obligatory swapping through narrow gates. This is the recommended second prototype only after the toolkit-only baseline succeeds.

## Conflicting pressures to resolve explicitly

Permanent movement growth makes revisits exciting but increases the space of reachable geometry. A bigger jump can trivialise earlier obstacles; that can be an enjoyable mastery payoff, while unintentionally bypassing the only teaching encounter can hurt later comprehension. Separate required teaching from optional sequence breaks.

Restricted loadouts generate choice but can undermine embodiment: changing jump height repeatedly changes muscle memory. Keep baseline movement stable unless players clearly enjoy recalibration. Likewise, a quality-of-life glyph occupying scarce capacity may feel like choosing tolerable controls over interesting play. Collection convenience and navigation assistance deserve special scrutiny before being priced as strategic advantages.

Scarcity can make discovery valuable, but generic currency can turn an intriguing detour into bookkeeping. A relay repair funded by shards links collection to the world; a compulsory shard quota can instead turn exploration into grinding. Never require collecting nearly everything to advance the main story. Distinctive discoveries should sometimes grant the thing directly.

Coherence lore can justify attunement and interference, but terminology alone does not unify mechanics. A discovery should visibly change what the Signal does. Curses that mainly damage enjoyable movement conflict with the game's strength; optional clearly signposted challenge contracts are a better hypothesis than surprise penalties in ordinary rewards.

## Tests before balance numbers

1. **Toolkit-only comparison.** Reuse a short exploration loop with deterministic traversal discovery, an immediately usable obstacle and a shortcut back to a familiar landmark. Compare against the present reward flow with similar play time. Record voluntary detours, remembered destinations, interruptions and what players name as their best reward. Do not judge only by completion speed.
2. **Required-route audit.** Walk every mandatory gate using only guaranteed abilities, ignoring all optional shards, attunements and lucky offers. Include death immediately after an unlock, quitting after a shortcut opens, and returning with an earlier checkpoint. Check both accessibility and escape from reached areas.
3. **Optional loadout trial.** Give known attunements and free relay swaps in the same loop. Observe whether players articulate competing reasons, actually change configurations, and retain movement confidence. Compare with no loadout menu. A visible menu interaction is not itself evidence of worthwhile choice.
4. **Reward identity trial.** Compare a direct discovery, a visible repair requiring found shards, and a random offer at matched effort. Ask players to locate the reward on their mental map and explain what changed. This separates anticipation and spatial memory from pickup frequency.
5. **Late-game simulation.** Start players with a complete toolkit and familiar loadout. Test a new environmental interaction and a return route without promising another statistical upgrade. If the section feels empty, improve situations and consequences before expanding the catalogue.

The immediate research recommendation is architecture A as the exploration prototype, with C tested as an optional addition. Balance capacity and shard prices only after those tests establish what players are paying for.

A concrete low-risk hypothesis from the synthesis discussion is to reuse existing movement: discover permanent Dash in Listening Field and Air Jump later in Drowned Array. Levers, dish rotation and lift interaction can use a baseline context action. These locations and ordering remain a proposed experiment, not a live or approved change. New Pulse or Anchor light-manipulation verbs should compete as later prototypes rather than become essential gates before their play value is demonstrated. Persistent world saving and return connections between biomes are dependencies of this direction, not cosmetic polish after progression is finished.
