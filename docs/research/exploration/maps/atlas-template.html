A world that can answer

The Spark follows an unanswered maintenance call. A distant workshop window responds to its first repair. Reaching that place begins a story about learning what deserves to be reconnected—and how to make the repair last.

The payoff is a return: the workshop repair holds without the Spark beside it. Required clues sit on working routes; optional archives deepen the history without buying a hidden true ending. Read the cited story study.

World connections

After the tutorial, the Field workshop and the Drowned service route are independent leads. The Wire and freight lift offer different ways to reach the Array. The Gate closes the network's restoration without forcing every visit into one order.

Open connectionRequirement unmetPersistent shortcutOptional ability route

Flags can be combined freely to inspect dependencies; this is not a playable progression simulation. Solid routes are bidirectional once accessible. The Approach service return is opened from the Approach side, then works both ways. District access does not imply every internal room is reachable.

Reusable design blocks

Reuse a relationship, not an interchangeable piece of scenery. Black bars are schematic ground; dashed lines indicate routes or connections. Dimensions are planning envelopes in logical pixels, not tested jumps. Every block needs an authored exit and reset.

What is needed to repair each place?

Sometimes a part; sometimes an insight or a safe supply. Finding hardware should solve a remembered problem, not impose the same collection quota in every district.

DistrictRepair objective
Listening FieldReconnect, align and clear intact equipment.
The StandContain and redirect storm charge.
Drowned ArrayManually bleed the basin, recover the pump impeller, fit it on dry ground and power the guided float.
The WireRecognise the missing brake mount, find the spare in the inspection shelter, fit it and test recall.
The Array / GateReconfigure known machinery and apply the restored network; no arbitrary last component hunt.

Parts persist through death, take no ability slots and fit their matching machine through a contextual action. The first drain must work before the impeller is found, and the Wire spare must be reachable without its broken carriage.

Puzzle state models

Choose a step to inspect its contract. These states specify what the player should understand and what persists. They do not simulate physics or prove that all controls remain reachable.

Climbing, traps and hazards

Wall slide and wall kick already exist in the controller. Use them for service shafts with resting shelves and memorable upper exits. Contextual ladders are a proposed experiment; ledge grabbing and free climbing remain undecided.

Teach one uncertainty at a time. Darkness and weather cannot conceal the landing, alter a hazard's timing or replace its warning. The first slice tests climbing and a harmless plate before combining them with danger.

First build and evaluation

Build one place that remembers

  1. A separate exploration save and a two-way room transition.
  2. Triangulation Hub with two visible leads.
  3. West Workshop, Ballast Gallery and Upper Amplifier.
  4. Permanent Dash, safe immediate practice and a remembered opportunity.
  5. Counterweight Return and a saved hub lift.
  6. A lower Drowned lookout, one replayable memory and one restored window pattern.

Observe before expanding

  • Can players name two useful leads?
  • Can they predict the lever's result?
  • Do they recognise the return location?
  • Do discoveries survive death and reload?
  • Does every machine state have an escape?
  • Do muted audio, grayscale and reduced effects preserve essential cues?
